**TECHNOLOGY & SECURITY**

All right, so let's talk about something that should genuinely concern you if you use cryptocurrency or have any crypto-related accounts: Microsoft has discovered a new lightweight backdoor malware that they're calling "Crypto Clipper," and it does exactly what the name suggests—it steals cryptocurrency.

Here's how it works: the malware spreads via USB, which is kind of old-school, but it's also effective because people still use USB drives. And here's the clever part—it communicates over Tor, which makes it harder to trace back to whoever's controlling it. The whole setup is designed to be lightweight, meaning it doesn't take up much space and doesn't necessarily trigger all the obvious security alarms. What Crypto Clipper does is intercept cryptocurrency transactions. So if you copy a wallet address, it swaps in a different one—the attacker's address—so when you paste it to send your funds, you're actually sending them to someone else.

This is one of those things where the technical sophistication is modest, but the attack surface is huge because so many people are moving crypto around and copy-pasting addresses. And USB transmission is actually pretty effective at spreading because people share USB drives, use them at work, at home, on different machines. 

So look: if you're in the crypto space at all, make sure your machines have updated security software, don't just blindly plug in USB drives, and honestly, double-check wallet addresses before you hit send. I know that seems paranoid, but given that there's active malware designed to trick you into exactly that... it's just good hygiene.
